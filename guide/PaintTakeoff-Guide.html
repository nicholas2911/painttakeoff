PaintTakeoff — Quick Guide

Measure your painting plans right on screen. No training needed.

First time opening the app

Double-click PaintTakeoff-0.1.0-portable.exe. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" message — that's normal for a new app. Click More info → Run anyway. Nothing is installed; the file is the whole program.

1 Open a plan

Click the big Open a Plan (PDF) button and pick the PDF your architect or GC sent you — or just drag the file anywhere onto the window.

Use the ◀ ▶ buttons (or your arrow keys) to move between pages. Most painting work is on the floor plans (the A-series sheets).

2 Set the scale

The app needs one real-world length before it can measure. Do this once per page:

  • Click Set Scale.
  • Click the two ends of anything whose length you know — a wall with its dimension written on it is perfect. Longer lines give better accuracy.
  • Type the real length into the Feet and Inches boxes (fractions like 6½ are fine) and hit Set Scale.

If the plan already prints its scale, you can skip the clicking: pick it from the Common scales… dropdown instead (most house plans are ¼ inch = 1 foot).

The app then offers a quick double-check: measure one more known length, ideally pointing the other direction. It takes 10 seconds and catches stretched or mislabeled plans. Skip it if you're in a hurry.

Watch the badge at the top of the screen:

Scale is set ✓ good to go  ·  ⚠ Scale not confirmed works, but double-check when you can

Remembered for you: the scale is saved per page, per file. Close the app and reopen the same plan tomorrow — your scale is still there.

3 Measure

Click Measure, then click to start a line and keep clicking to add segments — wall to wall, around obstacles. The running total follows your cursor. Double-click or Enter to finish. In a hurry, click-drag still works for a quick single line.

Finished measurements stay on the page — flip to another page and come back, they're still there (even after closing the app). Click one to select it; Delete removes it. Right-click while drawing undoes your last point.

Open Measurements in the toolbar for the list of everything on the page — rename any line, delete any line, and read the total at the bottom.

Quick Area — rough room sizes in one click

Once the scale is set, click Quick Area and then click inside a room. The app fills the room and gives you a rough floor area and perimeter. Type the ceiling height and it shows the rough wall square footage — the number you actually price off.

  • Every number on the card is editable — tweak anything that looks off.
  • Cut out an obstacle subtracts things like cabinet blocks or islands.
  • Keep this room saves it into your Measurements list.
It's "rough" on purpose: rooms with wide-open doorways can leak into the next room, and dashed-line rooms may fill in pieces. If a fill looks wrong, cancel and chain-measure the walls with the Measure tool instead.

Moving around the page

Zoom — Ctrl + mouse wheel, or the − / + buttons

Pan — drag with the mouse in Move Around mode, or hold Space and drag

Fit the page — Fit ▾ menu in the zoom cluster

Pages — arrow keys, or type a page number

Cancel anything — Esc or right-click

Modes — V move · C set scale · M measure

Good habits

  • Every page has its own scale. A set can mix ¼", ⅛", and metric sheets — check the badge each time you flip pages.
  • Calibrate off the longest written dimension on the sheet, never off a short one — small reading errors shrink as the line gets longer.
  • Scanned plans stretch. If the double-check complains, recalibrate using a dimension running the other direction.
  • Feet-and-inches or meters — the toggle in the toolbar switches everything, including the input boxes.
  • The amber badge is a button. If a page says "Scale not confirmed — fix", click it any time to run the double-check and turn it green.
  • Need an unusual scale? "Common scales… → Custom…" lets you type any ratio (1:60) or inches-per-foot (3/16" = 1').
